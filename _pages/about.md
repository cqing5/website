I'm a first-year PhD student in Psychology at UC San Diego. 

I graduated from Stanford University with my [M.S. in Symbolic Systems](https://symsys.stanford.edu/graduates/masters-program), an interdisciplinary program combining several fields, including computer science, psychology, and linguistics. At Stanford, I worked with [Hyowon Gweon](https://sll.stanford.edu) to investigate adaptive communication in children and children's social networks. I also worked with Hazel Markus and Jennifer Eberhardt at [Stanford SPARQ](https://sparq.stanford.edu) to apply computational methods to investigate how people understand and respond to inequality.

Before I came to Stanford, I earned a B.S. in Psychology (honors) and a B.S. in Informatics from Indiana University, where I also received a Certificate in Clinical Psychological Science. As an undergraduate, I studied pain perceptions of low-socioeconomic status individuals with [Kurt Hugenberg](https://hugenberg.lab.indiana.edu/index.html). 

## Research Interests
 I am broadly interested in how we come to reason about the flow and transfer of information, particularly through communication and gossip. For example, how do we develop the social-cognitive and evidential reasoning skills that allow us to infer others’ intentions, beliefs, and traits from what they communicate and the information they choose to share? More specifically, I investigate how social cognition intersects with statistical reasoning about sampling and representativeness, and how these processes are related to selective trust, epistemic vigilance, deception, and reputation formation. I aim to investigate both sides of the communicative equation: how people produce and curate evidence to shape others’ beliefs and how consumers may interpret and/or discount such  evidence. I am particularly interested in how these processes unfold in interactions with both humans and AI systems, including how people evaluate or interpret AI-generated explanations, respond to errors and agreement, and update their beliefs about the reliability of a source. 
 
 I value taking an interdisciplinary approach to research and combining perspectives and concepts from psychology, cognitive science, linguistics, and computational modeling.
