## High School
I believe that early, hands‑on exposure to AI/ML helps to demystify these tools, broadens access to computational careers, and helps young learners develop the technical and ethical fluency needed to use these tools responsibly. As a result, I've worked to bring AI into pre‑college classrooms to support students in connecting abstract concepts to problems they care about. 

I have served as a Computer Science & Machine Learning Instructor at St. George’s School (Vancouver) and The Harker School (Bay Area), delivering practical, project‑based instruction in Python and core ML methods to cohorts of 30+ students. This included covering foundational topics like linear and logistic regression, as well as applied areas like natural language processing and computer vision. I loved watching students train their first models and progress to more advanced architectures. My favorite part was getting to mentor student teams on end‑to‑end projects—e.g., training computer‑vision models for disease detection—guiding them through data collection, model development, evaluation, and presentation of  results.

I’ve also led virtual and in‑person workshops for high‑school students and K–12 teachers on integrating AI tools and agents into classrooms. These sessions focus on hands‑on activities and practical strategies for using AI to support learning, with the goal of empowering both educators and students to adopt responsible, effective AI practices.

## Stanford
One of the highlights of my time at Stanford was serving as a Graduate Teaching Assistant for SYMSYS 200: Minds and Machines, taught by Dr. Noah Goodman and Dr. Jiajun Wu (for four quarters!). Due to the course's flipped classroom structure, most of students' learning occurred in our weekly discussion sections, where I focused on creating an engaging, inclusive learning environment. Importantly, this role gave me the opportunity to make topics like AI and neural networks accessible to students of all academic and personal backgrounds. I also had the opportunity to TA the course for Stanford Summer Session, where teaching high school and university students from all over the world for the first time was both challenging and deeply rewarding, and strengthened my ability to communicate complex ideas to new audiences. ## IU
### Human-Computer Interaction (HCI) Courses
At IU, I was an Associate Instructor for two foundational HCI courses. In Spring 2022, I taught INFO-I 246: Use and Usability with Dr. Phil Jordan, and in Fall 2021, I taught INFO-I 300: HCI/Design with Dr. Katie Siek. Both roles allowed me to mentor and guide studios of 20-30 students through various stages of the design process. From conducting primary and secondary user research to conceptualizing designs and usability testing, I helped students develop their skills in wireframing and prototyping using tools like Figma and Adobe XD. This was an incredible opportunity to help foster students'  design thinking, user research skills, ability to navigate usability challenges, and creativity in developing user-centered solutions.

### Psychology Courses
In addition to HCI courses, I also TA'd for several psychology courses, ranging from large introductory courses to advanced capstone courses:
* PSY-P 304: Social Psychology with Dr. Dorainne Green (Spring 2022)
* PSY-P 101: Introductory Psychology with Dr. Timothy Patrick (Fall 2021)
* PSY-P 421: Laboratory in Social Psychology with Dr. Timothy Patrick (Fall 2020 – Spring 2021)
* PSY-P 325: Psychology of Learning with Dr. Timothy Patrick (Spring 2020)

 While each course had its unique aspects, one of my favorite experiences was TAing the capstone course, PSY-P 421. In this course, I guided students through the research process, from refining their independent research questions to designing their studies and conducting their analyses. I loved helping students develop their own research projects and getting to combine my passion for research and teaching.
